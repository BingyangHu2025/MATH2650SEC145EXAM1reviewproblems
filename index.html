Problem 1

Exam 1 Review Session
Linear first-order IVP
Solve the initial value problem \[ (x^2+1)y'+4x^3y=8xe^{-2x^2},\qquad y(0)=1. \]
Step 0 — Standard form. Divide by \(x^2+1\): \[ y'+\frac{4x^3}{x^2+1}y=\frac{8xe^{-2x^2}}{x^2+1}. \]
Step 1 — Find \(P,Q\) and the integrating factor. \[ P(x)=\frac{4x^3}{x^2+1},\qquad Q(x)=\frac{8xe^{-2x^2}}{x^2+1}. \] Also, \[ \int P(x)\,dx=2x^2-2\ln(x^2+1), \] so \[ \rho(x)=\frac{e^{2x^2}}{(x^2+1)^2}. \]
Step 2 — Apply the linear-equation formula. \[ y(x)=\frac1{\rho(x)}\left[\int \rho(x)Q(x)\,dx+C\right]. \] This reduces to \[ y=(x^2+1)^2e^{-2x^2}\left[\int\frac{8x}{(x^2+1)^3}\,dx+C\right]. \]
Step 3 — Use the initial condition. After integration, \[ y=e^{-2x^2}\left[-2+C(x^2+1)^2\right]. \] Now substitute \(y(0)=1\).

Problem 2

Exam 1 Review Session
Substitution → separable
Solve \[ y'=(3x+y-1)^2. \]
Step 1 — Standard form. The equation is already solved for \(y'\). Notice the repeated expression \(3x+y-1\).
Step 2 — Substitute. Let \[ v=3x+y-1. \]
Step 3 — Replace \(y,y'\). Since \(y=v-3x+1\), \[ y'=v'-3. \] Hence \[ v'-3=v^2,\qquad \frac{dv}{v^2+3}=dx. \]
Step 4 — Solve \(v\), then recover \(y\). Integrate \[ \int\frac{dv}{v^2+3}=\int dx \] and substitute back \(v=3x+y-1\).

Problem 3

Exam 1 Review Session
Exact equation
Solve, where the equation is defined, \[ y'=-\frac{2xy+y^2+y}{x^2+2xy+x}. \]
Step 1 — Differential form. \[ (2xy+y^2+y)\,dx+(x^2+2xy+x)\,dy=0. \] Thus \[ M=2xy+y^2+y,\qquad N=x^2+2xy+x. \]
Step 2 — Test exactness. \[ M_y=2x+2y+1,\qquad N_x=2x+2y+1. \] Therefore \(M_y=N_x\).
Step 3 — Build the potential. \[ F=\int M\,dx+g(y)=x^2y+xy^2+xy+g(y). \]
Step 4 — Determine \(g\). Use \(F_y=N\): \[ x^2+2xy+x+g'(y)=x^2+2xy+x, \] so \(g'(y)=0\).
Step 5 — Conclude. Absorb the constant \(g(y)\) into the arbitrary constant and write \(F(x,y)=C\).

Problem 4

Exam 1 Review Session
Homogeneous equation
Solve, for \(x\ne0\), \[ xy\,y'=2x^2+3y^2. \]
Step 1 — Standard form. \[ y'=\frac{2+3(y/x)^2}{y/x}. \] This depends only on \(y/x\).
Step 2 — Substitute. \[ v=\frac yx. \]
Step 3 — Replace \(y,y'\). Since \(y=xv\), \[ y'=v+xv'. \] Thus \[ xv'=\frac{2(1+v^2)}v,\qquad \frac{v}{1+v^2}\,dv=2\frac{dx}{x}. \]
Step 4 — Integrate and recover \(y\). \[ \frac12\ln(1+v^2)=2\ln|x|+C. \] Exponentiate and substitute \(v=y/x\).

Problem 5

Exam 1 Review Session
Bernoulli equation
Solve, for \(x\ne0\), \[ xy'+2y=x^2y^3. \]
Step 1 — Bernoulli standard form. \[ y'+\frac2x y=xy^3. \] Thus \(P=2/x,\ Q=x,\ n=3\).
Step 2 — Substitute. \[ v=y^{1-n}=y^{-2}. \]
Step 3 — Solve the linear equation for \(v\). \[ v'-\frac4xv=-2x. \] Its integrating factor is \[ \rho=x^{-4}. \] Solving gives \[ v=x^2+Cx^4. \]
Step 4 — Recover \(y\). Since \(v=y^{-2}\), \[ y^{-2}=x^2+Cx^4. \] Also check \(y\equiv0\) directly in the original equation.

Bernoulli equation
Solve \[ y^3y'+xy^4=4x. \]
Step 1 — Bernoulli standard form. For nonzero \(y\), \[ y'+xy=4xy^{-3}. \] Thus \(P=x,\ Q=4x,\ n=-3\).
Step 2 — Substitute. \[ v=y^{1-n}=y^4. \]
Step 3 — Solve the linear equation for \(v\). \[ v'+4xv=16x. \] The integrating factor is \[ \rho=e^{2x^2}. \] Hence \[ v=4+Ce^{-2x^2}. \]
Step 4 — Recover \(y\). Since \(v=y^4\), take real fourth roots and state the interval condition.

Step 5 — Conclude. Substitute \(g\) into \(F\) and set \(F=C\).

Exact equation
Solve \[ (e^x\sin y+\tan y)\,dx+(e^x\cos y+x\sec^2y+2y)\,dy=0. \]
Step 1 — Identify \(M,N\). \[ M=e^x\sin y+\tan y,\qquad N=e^x\cos y+x\sec^2y+2y. \]
Step 2 — Test exactness. \[ M_y=e^x\cos y+\sec^2y,\qquad N_x=e^x\cos y+\sec^2y. \]
Step 3 — Build the potential. \[ F=\int M\,dx+g(y)=e^x\sin y+x\tan y+g(y). \]
Step 4 — Determine \(g\). \[ F_y=e^x\cos y+x\sec^2y+g'(y)=N, \] so \(g'(y)=2y\) and \(g(y)=y^2\).
Step 5 — Conclude. Substitute \(g(y)=y^2\) and set \(F=C\).

Problem 9

Exam 1 Review Session
Homogeneous equation
Solve \[ (x^2+xy+y^2)\,dx-x(x+2y)\,dy=0. \]
Step 1 — Standard form. \[ y'=\frac{1+y/x+(y/x)^2}{1+2y/x}. \] This depends only on \(y/x\).
Step 2 — Substitute. \[ v=\frac yx. \]
Step 3 — Replace and separate. Since \(y=xv\), \[ xv'=\frac{1-v^2}{1+2v}. \] Before dividing, note the constant solutions \(v=\pm1\), corresponding to \(y=\pm x\). For \(v\ne\pm1\), \[ \frac{1+2v}{1-v^2}\,dv=\frac{dx}{x}. \]
Step 4 — Partial fractions and recovery. \[ \frac{1+2v}{1-v^2} =\frac{3/2}{1-v}-\frac{1/2}{1+v}. \] Integrate, substitute \(v=y/x\), and simplify.

Problem 10

Exam 1 Review Session
Second order: x missing
Solve \[ y''=2y(y')^3. \]
Step 1 — Identify the missing variable. The independent variable \(x\) does not appear explicitly.
Step 2 — Substitute. \[ p=y',\qquad y''=p\frac{dp}{dy}. \]
Step 3 — Solve \(p(y)\). \[ p\frac{dp}{dy}=2yp^3. \] For nonconstant solutions \(p\ne0\), \[ \frac{dp}{p^2}=2y\,dy. \] Thus \[ p=-\frac1{y^2+C_1}. \]
Step 4 — Recover \(y\). Since \(p=dy/dx\), \[ \frac{dx}{dy}=-(y^2+C_1). \] Integrate with respect to \(y\). Also check constant solutions separately.

Problem 12

Exam 1 Review Session
Second order: x missing
Solve \[ yy''+(y')^2=yy'. \]
Step 1 — Identify the missing variable. The independent variable \(x\) is missing explicitly.
Step 2 — Substitute. \[ p=y',\qquad y''=p\frac{dp}{dy}. \]
Step 3 — Solve \(p(y)\). \[ yp\frac{dp}{dy}+p^2=yp. \] For \(p\ne0\), \[ y\frac{dp}{dy}+p=y, \qquad \frac{dp}{dy}+\frac1y p=1. \] This is linear in \(p(y)\), with integrating factor \(\rho(y)=y\).
Step 4 — Recover \(y\). \[ \frac{d}{dy}(yp)=y, \qquad p=\frac y2+\frac{C_1}{y}. \] Since \(p=dy/dx\), separate variables and integrate.
